\begin{abstract}
Few-shot cross-domain class-incremental learning introduces novel classes from distinct visual domains with only a handful of labelled support images per class. HyCal scores queries on frozen CLIP features by combining cosine similarity with per-class Mahalanobis distance; however, with $K \in \{5, 10\}$ support images per class in a 512-dimensional fused feature space, per-class sample covariances are severely rank-deficient. We propose replacing each class covariance with a convex combination of itself (25\%) and the session-level pooled within-class covariance (75\%), leaving prototypes, image--text fusion, calibration, and weighting unchanged. In paired comparisons changing only the shrinkage coefficient $\alpha$, mean test accuracy rises by $+2.93$ and $+2.92$ percentage points (pp) on EuroSAT RGB (5/10-shot) and $+1.71$ and $+4.00$ pp on Galaxy10 DECaLS, improving 11 of 12 runs. Transferred without retuning to held-out Flowers102 and OrganAMNIST, shrinkage improves all 12 runs ($+2.15$ pp on Flowers102; $+4.92$ and $+6.02$ pp on OrganAMNIST). We also show that the official balanced script's threshold ($\nmin = 20$) reduces HyCal to cosine-only scoring for $K \le 10$, and that HyCal's shot-dependent weighting prevents catastrophic cross-domain misrouting under unequal shot counts while preserving shrinkage gains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Few-shot class-incremental learning expands a classifier over sequential sessions using only a few labelled support images per class, while requiring inference across all seen classes in a single shared label space. In the cross-domain setting, successive sessions introduce visually disparate domains---such as satellite imagery, astronomical surveys, botanical photography, and medical scans---and predictions must be made solely from the query image without domain identifiers.

HyCal \citep{lee2026hycal} addresses this setting without backbone fine-tuning by keeping CLIP \citep{radford2021clip} frozen and storing a prototype and sample covariance for each class in an image--text fused space. At test time, it calibrates cosine similarity and Mahalanobis distance across candidate classes and blends them via a shot-dependent weight. Because each session appends per-class statistics without modifying prior ones, HyCal avoids feature drift and requires no replay buffer.

A central bottleneck arises in the low-shot regime ($K \in \{5, 10\}$): each per-class sample covariance $S_c$ lies in $\mathbb{R}^{512 \times 512}$ yet has rank at most $K$, leaving roughly 500 feature directions governed purely by an isotropic ridge term $\lambda I$. As a result, the estimated precision matrix is poorly conditioned and sensitive to sampling noise. Drawing on classical covariance shrinkage \citep{friedman1989,ledoit2004}, we investigate pooling within-class residuals across all classes within the current session and shrinking each class covariance toward this shared estimate. Unlike methods that modify the encoder or require stored support images from earlier sessions, session-level shrinkage adds a single scalar hyperparameter $\alpha$ and integrates directly into HyCal's existing incremental workflow.

Our study makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Session-level pooled covariance shrinkage for HyCal.} We formulate a single-parameter shrinkage estimator inside HyCal's fused space and demonstrate consistent paired gains across four distinct visual domains (23 of 24 five- and ten-shot runs), including zero-retuning transfer to held-out natural and medical domains.
\item \textbf{Controlled evaluation against strong baselines and alternative designs.} We identify that the official balanced script (\texttt{run\_balanced.sh}, $\nmin = 20$) disables Mahalanobis scoring for $K \le 10$, construct properly tuned controls that isolate $\alpha$ alone, and compare shrinkage against two alternative scoring modifications---projected shared visual covariance and query-gated weighting---on identical EuroSAT splits.
\item \textbf{Per-class and cross-domain incremental analysis.} We show that shrinkage improves 9/11 medical organ classes at 5-shot and all 11/11 at 10-shot (and 62/102 flower classes at 5-shot), and analyze a two-domain unequal-shot failure mode ($K = 10/1$) where covariance scale mismatch causes 42.18\% cross-domain misrouting under Mahalanobis-only scoring, whereas weighted HyCal with shrinkage keeps cross-domain error at or below 0.11\%.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Method}

\subsection{HyCal Preliminaries}

Given a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder \citep{radford2021clip}, an image is mapped to a unit vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ and each candidate class prompt to a unit vector $t_c \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$. HyCal \citep{lee2026hycal} constructs a candidate-conditioned fused representation:
\begin{equation}
e_c = \frac{v + t_c}{\lVert v + t_c \rVert}.
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
For class $c$ with $K_c$ support images in a session, HyCal computes fused support vectors $\{e_{c,i}\}_{i=1}^{K_c}$, stores the normalised class prototype $p_c = \bar{e}_c / \lVert \bar{e}_c \rVert$ where $\bar{e}_c = \frac{1}{K_c}\sum_{i=1}^{K_c} e_{c,i}$, and estimates the sample covariance centered on $p_c$:
\begin{equation}
S_c = \frac{1}{K_c - 1} \sum_{i=1}^{K_c} (e_{c,i} - p_c)(e_{c,i} - p_c)^\top \quad (K_c > 1),
\label{eq:cov}
\end{equation}
falling back to $S_c = 10^{-3} I$ when $K_c = 1$ \citep{hycalcode}.

At inference, each candidate class receives a cosine score and a regularised Mahalanobis score with default ridge $\lambda = 10^{-4}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
s^{\mathrm{cos}}_c &= \cos(e_c, p_c),\\
s^{\mathrm{md}}_c &= -(e_c - p_c)^\top (S_c + \lambda I)^{-1} (e_c - p_c).
\end{split}
\label{eq:scores}
\end{equation}
The official Relative Mahalanobis Distance adds a query-specific background term that is identical across all candidate classes for a given query, so it cancels out during per-query $z$-normalisation. Each score vector is $z$-normalised across all seen candidate classes, mapped through the standard Gaussian CDF $\Phi$, and combined via a shot-dependent weight:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{score}_c &= (1 - w_c)\,\Phi(z^{\mathrm{cos}}_c) + w_c\,\Phi(z^{\mathrm{md}}_c),\\
w_c &= \mathbb{I}[K_c \ge \nmin]\,\mathrm{sigmoid}\!\left(\frac{K_c - \nmin}{\mathrm{scale}}\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:combine}
\end{equation}
Classes with $K_c < \nmin$ receive $w_c = 0$ and are scored purely by cosine similarity.

\subsection{Pooled Covariance Shrinkage}

When $K_c \in \{5, 10\}$ and $d = 512$, $S_c$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov} has rank at most $K_c$, leaving $\sim 500$ orthogonal directions controlled solely by the ridge scalar $\lambda$. Consequently, sample noise in a handful of support images distorts the inverted precision matrix.

To stabilise covariance estimation without conflating class means, we pool centered within-class residuals across all multi-shot classes $\mathcal{C}_t^{+} = \{c \in \mathcal{C}_t : K_c > 1\}$ introduced in the current session $t$:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{pool}} = \frac{\sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t^{+}} \sum_{i=1}^{K_c} (e_{c,i} - p_c)(e_{c,i} - p_c)^\top}{\sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t^{+}} (K_c - 1)}.
\label{eq:spool}
\end{equation}
Centering each embedding by its own prototype $p_c$ removes between-class mean shifts while increasing the effective degrees of freedom from $K_c - 1$ to $\sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}_t^{+}} (K_c - 1)$ (raising the rank ceiling from $\le K$ to $\le C(K-1)$ in a balanced $C$-class session). Because pooling uses only classes arriving in session $t$, earlier sessions require no stored support images or retroactive updates.

We then shrink each class covariance toward $S_{\mathrm{pool}}$ before inversion to obtain the precision matrix:
\begin{equation}
P_c = \left[ (1 - \alpha)\, S_c + \alpha\, S_{\mathrm{pool}} + \lambda I \right]^{-1}, \quad \alpha \in [0, 1].
\label{eq:shrink}
\end{equation}
Per-class prototypes $p_c$, cosine scores, fusion, $z$-normalisation, and weighting remain identical to HyCal. For one-shot classes ($K_c = 1$), shrinkage is skipped and $w_c = 0$ under $\nmin = 2$, leaving one-shot predictions identical to cosine scoring.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter selection.}
We jointly tuned $(\alpha, \lambda, \nmin, \mathrm{scale})$ on the validation splits of EuroSAT and Galaxy10 by maximising mean accuracy across $K \in \{1, 5, 10\}$ and three support seeds. This selected $\alpha = 0.75$, $\lambda = 10^{-4}$, $\nmin = 2$, and $\mathrm{scale} = 1$ (under which $w_c \approx 1$ at $K \in \{5, 10\}$). Mean validation accuracy rose from $53.07\%$ at $\alpha = 0$ to $54.56\%, 54.62\%, 54.63\%$, and $54.49\%$ at $\alpha = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75$, and $1.0$, respectively. All four hyperparameters were frozen before primary test evaluation and held-out domain transfer. Setting $\alpha = 0$ with identical $(\lambda, \nmin, \mathrm{scale})$ yields the controlled baseline (\textit{Tuned HyCal}).

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets and protocol.}
Table~\ref{tab:data} summarises the four datasets spanning remote sensing, astronomy, fine-grained botany, and medical imaging. All runs use the same frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder with official evaluation preprocessing (bicubic resize to 224 px, 224-px center crop, CLIP normalisation, no augmentation), official prompt templates, and cached float32 L2-normalised embeddings. We verified exact prediction parity between our cached-feature implementation and the official \texttt{experiment\_runner.py} \citep{hycalcode} on all 8,100 EuroSAT 5-shot test images (seed 0) across cosine, Mahalanobis, and weighted scoring.

Support sets are sampled from each dataset's training split for $K \in \{1, 5, 10\}$ using three fixed seeds $\{0, 1, 42\}$ shared identically across all methods. We report top-1 test accuracy (mean $\pm$ sample std over three seeds) and paired seed-wise accuracy gains in percentage points (pp). Table~\ref{tab:configs} lists all scoring configurations alongside pure cosine-only and Mahalanobis-only baselines. With cached features, session fitting and full test-set scoring execute on CPU in under 0.5 s per run.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Primary Domains: EuroSAT and Galaxy10}

Table~\ref{tab:main} presents the primary evaluation across scoring baselines and pooled covariance shrinkage.

\paragraph{Official script threshold.}
Because \texttt{run\_balanced.sh} sets $\nmin = 20$, $w_c$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:combine} evaluates to zero for every $K \le 10$, making the Official configuration identical to cosine-only scoring. Switching to the CLI default ($\nmin = 5$, Baseline) activates Mahalanobis scoring and immediately raises EuroSAT accuracy by $+2.72$ pp (5-shot) and $+6.40$ pp (10-shot), and Galaxy10 by $+0.62$ and $+1.06$ pp. Zero-shot CLIP reaches $38.67\%$ on EuroSAT but collapses to $9.81\%$ on Galaxy10 (assigning 92\% of queries to a single galaxy class), underscoring the large domain gap.

\paragraph{Effect of pooled covariance shrinkage.}
Against the tightly matched control (Tuned HyCal, $\alpha = 0$), setting $\alpha = 0.75$ raises mean test accuracy in all four 5-/10-shot settings and improves 11 of 12 paired runs. On EuroSAT, shrinkage adds $+2.93 \pm 1.24$ pp at 5-shot (reaching $79.53\%$, a total $+8.16$ pp gain over Official, of which $+5.23$ pp comes from threshold/scale tuning and $+2.93$ pp from shrinkage) and $+2.92 \pm 0.25$ pp at 10-shot ($86.40\%$), winning all seeds. On Galaxy10, unshrunk Mahalanobis-only and Tuned HyCal slightly underperform Cosine-only ($37.99\%$ vs.\ $37.99\%$ at 5-shot; $39.74\%$ vs.\ $40.00\%$ at 10-shot), confirming that per-class covariances are noisy on this domain. Adding shrinkage resolves this degradation at 10-shot, delivering the largest primary gain ($+4.00 \pm 1.08$ pp, 3/3 seeds, reaching $43.74\%$). At Galaxy10 5-shot, mean accuracy rises by $+1.71 \pm 2.76$ pp, with one of three seeds decreasing.

\subsection{Zero-Retuning Transfer to Held-Out Domains}

To test whether the shrinkage gain overfits EuroSAT and Galaxy10, we applied the frozen configuration ($\alpha = 0.75, \lambda = 10^{-4}, \nmin = 2, \mathrm{scale} = 1$) without retuning to Flowers102 (102 fine-grained natural classes) and OrganAMNIST (11 medical CT classes). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:transfer}, shrinkage improves \textbf{all 12 of 12} paired runs. On Flowers102, accuracy increases by $+2.15$ pp at both 5-shot ($93.26\% \to 95.41\%$) and 10-shot ($95.02\% \to 97.17\%$) with low variance ($\le 0.36$ pp). On OrganAMNIST, where CLIP faces a severe grayscale medical domain shift, shrinkage yields substantial gains of $+4.92 \pm 0.59$ pp at 5-shot ($59.37\% \to 64.30\%$) and $+6.02 \pm 1.18$ pp at 10-shot ($65.61\% \to 71.63\%$).

\subsection{Ablation and Alternative Scoring Designs}

\paragraph{Factor ablation and $\alpha$ sensitivity.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates threshold/scale tuning, stronger isotropic regularisation ($\lambda: 10^{-4}\to 10^{-3}$), and pooled shrinkage. Lowering $\nmin$ and $\mathrm{scale}$ increases reliance on Mahalanobis scoring, which helps EuroSAT ($+2.51 / +1.42$ pp) but hurts Galaxy10 ($-0.62 / -1.32$ pp) when covariances are unshrunk. Increasing isotropic ridge $\lambda$ partially helps Galaxy10 ($+0.39 / +1.18$ pp) but leaves EuroSAT unchanged ($-0.04$ pp). By contrast, pooled shrinkage improves mean accuracy on both datasets at both ridge values, with $\lambda = 10^{-4}$ giving consistently larger gains because weaker isotropic damping preserves the directional covariance structure recovered by $S_{\mathrm{pool}}$. Results are also insensitive to the exact choice of $\alpha$: at $\lambda = 10^{-4}$, every $\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$ beats $\alpha = 0$ across all four settings (e.g., EuroSAT 5-shot $79.53\%$--$79.77\%$ vs.\ $76.60\%$; Galaxy10 10-shot $43.14\%$--$44.19\%$ vs.\ $39.74\%$).

\paragraph{Comparison with alternative scoring designs.}
We also evaluated two alternative scoring-only modifications on the exact same EuroSAT support sets and test split (Table~\ref{tab:alternatives}): (i) \textit{projected shared visual covariance}, which estimates a shared covariance in CLIP's visual space---akin to Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (GDA) \citep{wang2024gda}---and propagates it through Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion} (also tested on Flowers102, DTD \citep{cimpoi2014dtd}, and FGVC-Aircraft \citep{maji2013aircraft}, where gains over GDA were $+0.29 / +0.04 / +0.09$ pp); and (ii) \textit{query-gated weighting}, which boosts the Mahalanobis weight on queries with small top-2 cosine margin (also tested on OrganAMNIST, adding $+0.20 / +0.52$ pp over uniform boosting).

On EuroSAT, projected shared visual covariance and our pooled covariance shrinkage match within $0.22$ pp ($79.68\%$ vs.\ $79.53\%$ at 5-shot; $86.18\%$ vs.\ $86.40\%$ at 10-shot), while query-gated weighting trails by 4--6 pp and adds only $+0.27 / +0.69$ pp over uniformly scaling the weight ($73.60\%$ / $81.93\%$). This confirms that the dominant bottleneck in low-shot HyCal is \textbf{covariance estimation error} rather than per-query weight scheduling. Between the two covariance-sharing approaches, direct pooled shrinkage in the fused space achieves equivalent accuracy while keeping HyCal's native architecture intact and introducing only a single scalar parameter $\alpha$.

\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Per-Class Effects of Shrinkage and Error Regimes}

Table~\ref{tab:organ_perclass} breaks down the held-out OrganAMNIST accuracy by organ class (averaged over three seeds). Rather than concentrating on a single class, shrinkage provides broad gains: \textbf{9 of 11 organs improve at 5-shot} (led by Pancreas $+17.7$ pp, Left Lung $+7.3$ pp, and Left Femur $+6.3$ pp), and \textbf{all 11 of 11 organs improve at 10-shot} (with Left Lung $+14.7$ pp, Spleen $+11.5$ pp, and Right Kidney $+11.4$ pp). The same breadth holds on Flowers102: across 102 classes, shrinkage improves \textbf{62} classes (34 tied, 6 dropped) at 5-shot and \textbf{60} classes (36 tied, 6 dropped) at 10-shot.

Why does shrinkage work reliably across EuroSAT, Flowers102, OrganAMNIST, and Galaxy10 10-shot, yet show higher seed variance on Galaxy10 5-shot ($+1.71 \pm 2.76$ pp, 2/3 seeds)? Two factors interact:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pooled degrees of freedom.} At $K=5$ with $C=10$ classes, $S_{\mathrm{pool}}$ has only $C(K-1) = 40$ degrees of freedom in $\mathbb{R}^{512}$ (compared with $408$ on 102-class Flowers102 at 5-shot, or $90$ on Galaxy10 at 10-shot).
\item \textbf{Representation separability.} On EuroSAT, Mahalanobis scoring resolves visually confounded dark textures (reducing Forest $\to$ Sea/Lake errors across three seeds from 906 to 228, lifting Forest recall by $+27$ pp), leaving mainly fine-grained pairs (Annual vs.\ Permanent Crop, Industrial vs.\ Residential) constrained by $64\times 64$ upsampling. On Galaxy10, however, frozen CLIP features poorly separate subtle morphological classes (e.g., Disturbed and Merging galaxies stay at 20--27\% accuracy). With 40 noisy residuals at 5-shot, an unlucky support draw can perturb $S_{\mathrm{pool}}$; doubling to $K=10$ (90 degrees of freedom) stabilises the pooled covariance and unlocks a consistent $+4.00 \pm 1.08$ pp gain (3/3 seeds).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-Domain Incremental Evaluation}

We evaluated EuroSAT and Galaxy10 as a two-session incremental sequence (20 shared candidate classes in the final session) under both arrival orders and six shot combinations ($K_E / K_G \in \{5/5, 10/10, 10/1, 1/10, 10/5, 5/10\}$).

\paragraph{Equal shots and arrival order.}
At $K = 5/5$ and $10/10$, cross-domain misrouting is $0.00\%$ for all methods, and Shrinkage achieves $79.41\%$ / $86.42\%$ on EuroSAT and $39.93\%$ / $43.76\%$ on Galaxy10---matching single-domain accuracy within $0.25$ pp (the slight difference arises only because $z$-normalisation spans 20 classes instead of 10). Swapping arrival order produces identical final accuracies to two decimal places, and old-domain forgetting $F(d) = \max_u A(u, d) - A(T, d)$ is zero for cosine/Mahalanobis scoring and at most $0.21$ pp for weighted HyCal.

\paragraph{Unequal shots and covariance scale mismatch.}
Table~\ref{tab:cross} exposes a sharp failure mode when shot counts differ ($K_E = 10, K_G = 1$): under Mahalanobis-only scoring, $42.18\%$ of EuroSAT test images are misrouted to 1-shot Galaxy10 classes, dropping EuroSAT accuracy by $31.5$ pp (from $83.47\%$ to $51.93\%$). The cause is an $11\times$ variance scale mismatch: a 10-shot class has rank at most 10, so $\sim 500$ residual directions carry variance $\lambda = 10^{-4}$, whereas a 1-shot class uses the isotropic fallback $S_c + \lambda I = 1.1 \times 10^{-3} I$. Consequently, on EuroSAT queries, the median squared Mahalanobis distance to the nearest galaxy class (287) nearly matches that to the true EuroSAT class (274), even though cosine similarity cleanly separates them ($0.842$ vs.\ $0.978$).

HyCal's shot-dependent weighting resolves this mismatch because 1-shot classes receive $w_c = 0$ and fall back to cosine scoring. Combining weighted HyCal with pooled covariance shrinkage preserves this safeguard---keeping cross-domain misrouting at or below $0.11\%$ across all six shot combinations---while delivering the full $+2.92$ pp shrinkage advantage on EuroSAT ($86.36\%$ vs.\ $83.44\%$).

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Post-hoc ablations.} Primary hyperparameters were locked on the EuroSAT and Galaxy10 validation splits and transferred frozen to Flowers102 and OrganAMNIST; however, secondary ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) and error analyses were performed post-hoc on the test splits.
\item \textbf{Sample size of support seeds.} Using three fixed support seeds reveals consistent directional gains (23 of 24 five-/ten-shot runs) but limits statistical power for tight confidence intervals, particularly in high-variance regimes such as Galaxy10 5-shot.
\item \textbf{Incremental sequence length.} Our incremental evaluation tests two RGB domains (20 classes); longer multi-domain sequences with many more classes (e.g., eight domains in \citet{lee2026hycal}) may introduce calibration shifts across sessions not visible at two domains.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Shrinking each class covariance toward the session-level pooled within-class covariance ($\alpha = 0.75$) directly addresses covariance rank deficiency in few-shot HyCal without retraining CLIP or storing prior-session images. Under strictly matched controls, it improves 11 of 12 five-/ten-shot runs on EuroSAT and Galaxy10 (by $+2.93 / +2.92$ pp and $+1.71 / +4.00$ pp) and transfers without retuning to improve all 12 runs on held-out Flowers102 ($+2.15$ pp) and OrganAMNIST ($+4.92 / +6.02$ pp), lifting 9/11 to 11/11 medical organ classes. Combined with HyCal's shot-dependent weighting, pooled shrinkage matches more complex projected-covariance alternatives while keeping cross-domain misrouting at or below $0.11\%$ even under severe shot imbalance.
